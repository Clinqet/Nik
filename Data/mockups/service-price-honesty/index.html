Service price honesty — the minimum IS the price, and extras are disclosed

Customer web + phone, and the provider control that sets it (§5b). Today a service listed at $80 with a $120 minimum shows $80 on the card, tells Google $80 through structured data, and bills $120. The phone assistant already says it correctly. This sheet makes the screen catch up. Two separate fixes: (1) the headline price must never be lower than what will actually be charged; (2) extras that are added on top are flagged on the card and itemised on the page. Drawn for approval; nothing is built.

The defect, in one line. QuotePriceCalculator raises a total to minimumCharge, but servicePrice.js renders fixedPrice and ignores the minimum — and serviceOfferPrice hands Math.min(...) to schema.org. So we would understate the price on screen and declare the understated figure to a search engine.
‼️ HONEST SCOPE — corrected 2026-09-22. This is LATENT, not live. No provider UI sets minimumCharge today — verified, zero matches in either provider app. So the $80-shown / $120-billed case cannot happen through the product right now; it can only arrive by bulk import, seeding or a direct API call. But the data model carries it, QuotePriceCalculator honours it, and the phone assistant already states it — CatalogPriceNarrator's own comment says "No UI sets it yet, which is precisely why the branch is here with a test: the day one appears, the receptionist must not silently under-quote." The phone is already defended. The screen is not. This sheet defends the screen on the same terms, before a UI appears rather than after.
Price type and minimum charge are two different fields — this is the thing to be clear about. priceType is exactly one of Fixed, Range or Hourly (or nothing set) — a provider picks one, and only its own amounts apply. minimumCharge is a separate, optional field whose own definition in the entity is "Minimum total charge". It means "whatever the sum comes to, the bill will not be less than this", and the calculator applies it after any discount and before tax. So a provider could set Fixed $80 and minimum $120. They are not alternatives; the second is a floor under the first. In the table below, Stored is what their chosen price type holds and Minimum is that separate floor.
The two rules this sheet draws. RULE 1 — the headline is never below the real floor. When the minimum charge exceeds the computed headline, the minimum becomes the headline, everywhere: card, service page, provider page and structured data. RULE 2 — extras are flagged, not hidden. A charge that is added (a visit fee, a travel fee) gets a short flag on the card and its amount on the page. ‼️ Never a total, because a travel charge cannot be totalled until we know where the customer is.

Where these appear — every surface, not just the card. The search results page, the category and city landing pages, the recently viewed and trending rails, the service detail page, the provider Open Page, the cart and the schema.org Offer. ‼️ They must all read from the ONE formatter (utils/servicePrice.js) — it is already the only place a service price becomes a string, and that must stay true.
‼️ D46 — A DEFECT FOUND WHILE DRAWING THIS SHEET, and it is exactly the range problem. The C# side normalises the price type through NormalizePriceType(), which accepts four spellings — range, starting_from, startingfrom, starting from — and maps them all to range. servicePrice.js has no normaliser at all. It switches on the raw lower-cased string, so anything other than exactly fixed, range or hourly falls to default and renders fixedPrice || startPrice || minimumCharge — which silently drops the maximum and shows a single number where a range should be. The provider forms write "range", so form-created services are fine today. Anything from bulk import, seeding, a draft path or an older client is not. The fix is to normalise on the JS side with the same four spellings, and pin it with a test.

‼️ CAPITALISATION, BINDING — this is the app's existing convention, not a new one. A badge or pill is SENTENCE CASE — first word capitalised, nothing else. The customer app already does this everywhere: "First booking", "Action needed", "AI powered", "For you", "Expanding to your area". So it is "+ Visit fee", never "+ visit fee" and never "+ Visit Fee". An inline suffix beside a number is lowercase — the app already ships "search.taxIncluded": "tax included". So it is "$120 minimum" and "$40 / hour", lowercase. ‼️ The distinction is what the element IS, not where it sits: a bordered pill is a badge; loose words next to a figure are a suffix. Every new string on this sheet follows one of those two rules, and the copy table below marks which.
Card rules, binding. ‼️ The flag NEVER carries an amount — a travel charge cannot be totalled until we know the customer's distance, so a number on the card would be false precision. Amounts live on the page. One flag maximum, joined with the locale's own "and". A zero or unset amount is skipped, matching Enabled && HasValue. The flag is amber, never red — nothing is wrong, it is information. Money always through formatCurrency (currency + locale) — ‼️ never the phone narrator, which is deliberately currency-free.

5b · The provider control that sets it ‼️ provider web AND provider phone app · added 2026-09-22

‼️ THE APPROVED WORDING — owner-approved 2026-09-22. Use these exact words.

TITLE:   Set a minimum charge

HINT (web):   A customer is never charged less than this, even for a small job.

HINT (phone, shorter):   A customer is never charged less than this.

‼️ The hint names the CUSTOMER as the one charged. An earlier draft said "You will never be paid less than this" and the owner rejected it: the provider does not pay — the customer does. The hint also says why a provider would want it — "even for a small job" — so a $20 job does not cost more in travel than it earns. Keys: provider.price.minimum.label · provider.price.minimum.hint · provider.price.minimum.labelShort · provider.price.minimum.hintShort — all listed in §6.
Why this is here at all. Everything else on this sheet is the customer end. But no provider can set a minimum charge today — the field exists, the calculator honours it, the phone assistant states it, and there is simply no control. This frame closes that, so the feature is whole rather than half-built. Owned by PHASE 4, not Phase 3 — the rest of this sheet is Phase 3. See the traceability table, findings\PHASE-3-DESIGN-DECISIONS.md §1b.

What this sheet does NOT settle, and must not be assumed from it. The states drawn here are examples. The implementing session must work through every case it finds — a currency with no minor unit, a service whose minimum equals its price exactly, a range whose start and end are equal, a travel charge with a free allowance larger than any realistic distance, tax-inclusive versus tax-exclusive display, and anything else — and design for each one deliberately.
