5 · Disconnect is never blocked by how often you checked

What happened on 17–18 Sep. Connecting only saves your number and shows the codes — no call is placed, so no limit applies. Every check places a real call, and all checks shared one limit of five an hour — the disconnect check too. So after testing, "Disconnect my number" was refused while "connect" still worked. That limit is also counted separately on each of our servers and starts again after a restart, which is why some presses were refused and one went through. Nothing disconnects a number in the background: for a live assistant the only way out of own-number mode is a disconnect check that proves the forward is off — so one of your presses did start a check, and it finished after the window showing the refusal had stopped watching.

6 · What you are approving

R1 · the way out

Disconnecting gets its own hourly allowance, separate from checking

Five disconnect checks an hour, counted apart from the five for connecting and checking — so testing your line can never use up your way out. Leaving must never be harder than joining. "One check at a time" still applies.

R2 · the daily ceiling

It still counts toward the daily 15, and may go 5 past it

So a provider who spent all 15 testing can still leave. Why not unlimited: a check calls whatever number was typed in, and a number is typed in before anything proves it's theirs. With no ceiling, "type a stranger's number, disconnect, repeat" would ring that stranger as fast as the calls can finish. With these two, the worst case stays where it is today: five an hour, about 20 a day.

R3 · the messages

Every refusal says the real reason and what to do

Safe to say: it only describes the provider's own use, it's what every service does when it slows you down, and knowing the limit doesn't let anyone exceed it. Messages only need to stay vague when they would reveal something about someone else — like sign-in saying whether an email has an account. We still never show which of our numbers places the test call.

R4 · the card

Two links, and everything else where you'd look for it

"Check again · Adjust ring time" in the row. "Change" beside the number (except where the button already says "Check the number"). "Call your assistant to hear it" at the end of How it works. "No, I didn't pick up" inside the sentence it answers. Disconnect unchanged.

R5 · after a refusal

The window looks again before it shows an error

If a disconnect is refused, the window re-reads your assistant first. If the number is already off, it says so instead of an error — web and phone.

Not changed · deliberately

The hourly limit for connecting and checking stays

It's what stops a typed-in stranger's phone being called five times in five minutes. It is counted per server, so the wording says "within the hour" rather than promising an exact time we can't guarantee.
