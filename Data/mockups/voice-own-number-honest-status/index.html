00 · What was wrong — three you saw, five more the code showed

None of this needs anything new saved about a provider, and none of it adds a request. One check will actually make one request fewer than today.

You saw · 1

"About 15 seconds" after you chose 10

Not an old value left on screen. It is our test call's own stopwatch, from the moment we dial until the call reaches your assistant, so it also counts the few seconds before your phone starts ringing. 10 seconds of ringing plus that start-up rounds to 15. The screen labelled that number "your phone rings for", and it isn't.

Fix: say the ring time you chose (10), and show the test's stopwatch as its own line with the reason it's longer.

You saw · 2

The number field kept the old number until a reload

The settings form copies the number once, when the page opens. Worse than stale: saving any other setting afterwards sends that old number back, and the server refuses the whole save with "change it on your number card", right after you did exactly that.

Fix: while the field is locked it shows the number the card shows, from the same data. No extra request.

You saw · 3

Disconnect said "Waiting for your assistant"

And more than the last step: if you picked up during the disconnect check, or the check ran out of time, the screen said "The call reached your assistant, so the code didn't take". Neither happened. And "Missed calls go back to your voicemail" is promised before we know it.

Fix: every answer the disconnect check can give gets its own true sentence (§03).

Found in the code · 4

"Use a different number" with the same number reset the ring to 15

Continuing on step 1 with your own unchanged number quietly put the ring time back to the default, 15 seconds. That's the time your voicemail had just beaten. Code 2 on the next screen would have shown 15.

Fix: an unchanged number keeps the ring time you chose.

Found in the code · 5

The card kept saying "Shorten the ring" after you shortened it

Choosing 10 didn't clear the old "voicemail picked up first" result, so the card kept asking for a shorter ring until the next check. And on a connected line, changing the ring time made the card claim the new time before its code was even dialled.

Fix: a new ring time retires the voicemail result, and the card says "dial the new code, then check it" until a check proves it (§02).

Found in the code · 6

An answered or unfinished check turned a working line amber

If you picked up during "Check again", or no result came back at all, a connected line lost its green and could read "We didn't get the call. Usually this means code 1 didn't save". Neither result says anything about the line.

Fix: only a result that tested the line changes the card. "Answered" and "no result" leave it as it was.

Found in the code · 7

Pressing Stop hid the result for good

When a check finished after you pressed Stop or closed its window, the card behind stayed as it was until a reload. A pop-up "Checking your number" appeared after the check had already finished, even for a disconnect.

Fix: the finished check updates the card by itself, with one refresh when the result lands. No stale pop-up.

Found in the code · 8

After disconnecting, the screen promised voicemail it hadn't seen

Whether a voicemail answers after the disconnect code depends on the phone and the carrier. The check already tells us which happened (your voicemail answered, or the call rang out), but the screen always said "missed calls go to your voicemail again".

Fix: two "disconnected" screens, each saying what our test call actually found.

06 · Behind the screens — in plain words

  • Nothing new is saved about a provider. Every state on this sheet is decided from what the business already stores: the last result, the ring time, and the stopwatch from the last check that passed. No new table, field or setting.
  • One request fewer per check. When a result arrives live, the page reads the assistant once. Today it reads twice (once, then again 2.5 seconds later to be safe). The second read stays only where the result was found by asking again because the live update didn't arrive, since that is where it is needed. A check that finishes while its window is closed adds exactly one read, and only when the result lands. Nothing polls.
  • What the server does differently (five rules):
    • A check that ran out of time with no answer from the carrier changes nothing on the business. Today it marks the line "not connected, nothing arrived".
    • A check where the phone was answered changes nothing on the business. Today it takes a connected line off "connected".
    • A disconnect check that still reached the assistant records the line as connected, with today's date, because it just proved it. A disconnect check that couldn't reach the phone changes nothing.
    • Choosing a new ring time retires a "voicemail picked up first" result, because that result was about the old time. It can't be changed while a check call is live ("We're already checking your number"), so a result can never be measured against a time that changed mid-call.
    • Continuing with the same number in "Use a different number" keeps the chosen ring time if the carrier still offers it.
  • What a provider can no longer do: carry on at a ring time the voicemail just beat, from the check result, the card, the ring choice, or connect step 3. What is deliberately not remembered: once a shorter time has passed, the app no longer remembers that 15 lost. If someone later picks 15 again on purpose, the next check catches it and brings them straight back to 1B. Remembering it for ever would mean saving something new, and this sheet saves nothing new.
  • Every carrier, both apps, every width. Rogers and every GSM carrier get the ring choice; the Verizon family gets 1G and 2E. Laptop, iPad and phone web come from one layout; the provider app uses its own sheet, spinner, pull-to-refresh and haptics.
  • Unchanged by this sheet: the wizard's four steps, the pre-call confirm, "Looks like you answered" and "We couldn't reach that number" on connect, the offline banner, the "answers every call" states, region-off, and who may use these controls (a team member without "manage phone number" still can't).

07 · Decisions for you — nothing on this sheet is built until you say yes

Decision · 1

Voicemail first: a hard lock, or a strong recommendation?

Recommended: the lock drawn in 1C. The losing time and anything longer can't be picked in that flow, and there's no "Try again as is". A retry is only offered when nothing shorter exists (1F). A soft version would instead show 15 as "not recommended" and still let it through.

Decision · 2

Should new connections start at 10 seconds again?

Recommended: yes. 15 seconds lost to voicemail today on your own line. The plan measured that same line on 14 Sep and 15 won then. Voicemail timing moves a little from day to day, which is why the plan's original rule was "the highest time that wins, minus one step". 15 sits right on the edge. 15 would stay available in "Adjust ring time" for anyone who wants longer, and the check still catches it. This is the default in the admin Voice Settings page on both portals plus the built-in fallback. No provider already connected is changed.

Decision · 3

The five server rules in §06

They are what make the card honest after the window closes. Each gets unit and integration tests, and they apply to both regions' telephony (Canada/US and India) the same way.

Decision · 4

The words in §08

Every new or changed sentence, in English here. On approval they go into all five languages in both apps. One key is removed ("Try again as is").

09 · Rules this sheet is drawn to

  • A number on screen is labelled for what it measures. The ring time is the one chosen and dialled. The stopwatch is our test call's, and says so. Neither is ever presented as the other.
  • Only a result that tested the line changes the card. Arrived, voicemail first, rang out and couldn't reach are results. "You answered" and "we heard nothing" are not, and never move a line on or off green.
  • Every failure names its own fix, and a fix that can't work isn't offered. A retry at a ring time the voicemail just beat is not a fix.
  • One green button per screen. Amber is a problem to act on, grey is "we don't know", blue is a call in progress, red is disconnect. No new colours.
  • No technical words where a provider reads. Ring, code, voicemail, test call, check: nothing about how the check works inside.
  • Responsive, not redrawn. One dialog: centred on a laptop and iPad, a bottom sheet under 640 wide with its buttons stacked and the green one first. The locked option wraps instead of scrolling. Inputs stay 16px so iOS doesn't zoom.
  • The provider app mirrors the rendering rules, not the markup. Its own sheet, native spinner, the warning and success haptics, pull-to-refresh, tap-to-dial on Android and copy on iPhone.
  • Every sentence is a key in five languages in both apps, and every number in a sentence is one the server sent.
