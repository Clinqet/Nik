Rules your assistant always follows

The same idea your phone assistant already has, for the assistant your own team asks. Write the rules once; every answer to everyone in the business follows them.

They are business-wide and set by the owner or an administrator. Everyone else can read them, so nobody has to wonder why an answer keeps mentioning the deposit.

02b
Pick the wordingOne decision

The example line — three versions, drawn

This grey line under the box is the only teaching text most people ever read, and it has to work the same for a salon, an equipment dealer, a research firm and a two-hundred-person company. So it names no trade, no price, no booking and no day of the week. What every one of them does have is their own documents, figures that need a period attached, and documents that contradict each other.

What a rule can never do, and the panel says so in one line. It cannot invent a fact — every figure still comes from your own business's information, with its source beside it. And it cannot widen what anybody sees: a rule saying tell everyone the takings changes nothing for a member whose role does not cover money. If a rule asks for something that is not true or not permitted, the answer follows your business's information and says so, rather than following the rule.
The line in the panel
Rules shape how answers are writtenThey cannot create information that is not in your business, and they cannot show anyone more than their role allows.
Balanced is already chosen, and choosing it costs nothing. Balanced and Show figures as a table are exactly how Ask Clinket answers today, so a business that never opens this panel is answered identically to before — same words, same speed, same cost. Only Brief, Detailed, turning the table off, or writing a rule changes anything at all. Nobody ever sees an unset control, and nobody pays for a preference they did not express.

Drawn under the box, in the same quiet blue used elsewhere for "how this works". Without it, the first rule a provider writes will be one that cannot work, and they will read the result as a bug.

09
LimitsDecisions, written down

Five things this deliberately does not do

No personal rulesThese are the business's rules, and everyone's answers follow them. One person cannot set their own — which also means nobody can shorten only their own answers today. A personal layer can be added later without changing any of this.
No separate tone or greeting boxAsk Clinket talks to your own team and has no greeting to give. Anything you would put in a tone box is a rule, and belongs in the one box. The phone assistant keeps its own, because a call does open with a greeting.
Rules do not choose the languageAn answer comes back in whatever language the question was asked in. A rule saying otherwise is ignored, on purpose — being answered in a language you did not ask in is worse than a rule not applying.
Rules are not saved into conversationsThey apply when an answer is written. Changing them never rewrites an answer you have already been given, and reopening an old conversation shows what you were actually told at the time.
A history of changes is not keptThe panel says who last changed them and when. Who changed what, over time, is a bigger thing and is not part of this.
