Money in more than one currency

Four provider surfaces add up amounts that may not be in the same money. This sheet draws every state of each one, on the web and on the phone, and says where every word comes from. Nothing here changes when a business only ever takes one currency — that is the first frame of every pair, and it is exactly what ships today.

The rule this sheet applies. Amounts in different money are never added together. The screen leads with the total in the currency the business itself bills in, and lists any other money underneath, untouched. The symbol always belongs to the amount; the way the digits are grouped always belongs to the person reading.
Why this sheet exists. Today one provider's dashboard reads 233510.1 for a single day. That is ₹208,511.10 and C$24,999.00 added together — a number that is not any amount of money. It is live now on business LXDP8G.

5 · Where every state's words come from

A screen that re-invents a sentence the server already sends will drift out of translation. Every line above is one of these.

What the reader seesWhere the words come fromWhere the numbers come from
“Today's earnings”, “Paid · 12 invoices”, “Due this week”, “Earnings · last 7 days” The screen's own copy, already translated in all five languages. —
“Also paid today” / “Also paid” New copy on the screen, one key per app, added in all five languages. —
“Canadian dollars” and every other currency name New copy on the screen, one key per currency, in all five languages. Never a raw three-letter code — a provider should not have to know that CAD means Canadian dollars. The server names the currency; the screen turns it into words.
“Plus C$24,999.00 in Canadian dollars” New copy on the screen with two blanks — the amount and the currency name. The server sends each currency's own total separately.
“This chart shows Indian rupees. You were also paid C$24,999.00 in this period.” New copy on the screen with two blanks. Same.
“We could not load this just now.” · “No payments in this period yet.” · “Your role does not include earnings.” The screen's existing copy — unchanged by this sheet. —
Every amount: ₹208,511.10, C$24,999.00 — The symbol belongs to the money. The digits are grouped the way the reader's own language groups them — a Hindi reader sees ₹2,08,511.10 for the same amount.

6 · What does not change

  • A business that only ever takes one currency sees exactly what it sees today. Every "also" line, every extra row and every caption is absent unless a second currency really exists. That is the first frame of every pair above, and it is the overwhelming majority.
  • No count changes. "12 invoices" still means twelve invoices; only the money is separated.
  • Nothing is hidden. Money in another currency is always named and always shown in full. The one thing that stops happening is two currencies being added into a single number.
  • No new page, no new tap. Every state above is an existing tile, card, list heading or chart caption growing at most two lines.
