When a message does not arrive

Every screen the provider and the customer see when an email or a text message could not be delivered. Web and mobile, every state. The warning is always advice, never a block — nothing here stops anyone from sending.

0 · The rule every screen obeys

Three sentences decide every pixel below.

We never answer on the channel that broke

If the email bounced, we do not email about it. The notice arrives in the app and as a push. That is why the bell and the push are the guaranteed route, and why they cannot be switched off for this one kind of message.

We tell whoever can fix it

A customer's address that does not work is the provider's to correct — the customer cannot read the mail telling them so. A person's own account address is theirs, so they are told in the app.

It is advice, never a wall

Amber, never red. The provider can always send anyway. We are telling them what happened last time, not refusing to act.

2 · Before you send — the warning that prevents the failure

This is where the marker earns its keep. Because the record is kept against the address, we can warn the moment one is typed or pasted — before anything is sent, and even for an address this provider has never used.

Web · sending a quotetyped · warned · sent anyway
!

You are sending to an address that did not work last time. We will still send it.

It never blocks. "Send anyway" is the brand-green primary — the provider's judgement wins. We are the ones who know what happened last time; we are not the ones who decide.

Only the account holder can fix their own email. They are told in the app — never by email, which is the thing that failed.

5 · Locked out — the person we cannot reach at all

A password-reset email that does not arrive leaves someone with no way in. They cannot be emailed, cannot sign in, and have no phone registered. This screen is the whole remedy — not a notification, which is impossible here.

They stop waiting. Without this screen the person types the same address, sees "check your inbox", and waits for mail that will never come. ‼️ Support must be able to answer without email — a route that replies only by mail closes the loop back onto the broken channel.

6 · Responsive — phone, tablet, laptop, monitor

One layout, six behaviours. Breakpoints are the app's own (xs 320 · sm 480 · md 768 · lg 1024 · xl 1280 · 2xl 1536 · 3xl 1920), not invented for this sheet.

xs · 320–479

Phone, small

Contact row drops the address and keeps the pill. Advisory is full-bleed. Buttons stack, full width, 44px tall.

sm · 480–767

Phone, large

Address returns under the name on two lines. Advisory keeps its icon column.

md · 768–1023

Tablet portrait

Contact detail becomes two columns: identity left, advisory and fields right. Buttons go side by side.

lg · 1024–1279

Tablet landscape / small laptop

The list keeps a right-hand meta column (bookings, last seen). Advisory sits inline above the fields.

xl · 1280–1919

Laptop / desktop

List and detail side by side — selecting a contact never leaves the list. Advisory anchors to the detail pane.

3xl · 1920+

Large monitor

Content column caps at 1180px and centres. ‼️ It does not stretch — a 60-character line is the limit, whatever the monitor.

Touch is not hover. The apps set hoverOnlyWhenSupported, so every hover state here is wrapped in @media (hover:hover). On a phone or an iPad the amber pill must never latch into a stuck highlight after a tap.
The mobile apps are not a smaller web page. They use the native list row, the native sheet for the send-anyway confirmation, the OS push, and the bottom tab bar — with the same wording, the same amber, and the same rule that nothing ever blocks. Parity is in the rendering rules, not in sharing a component.

8 · What this sheet refuses to do

Never red

Red means stop, and nothing here stops. Amber is advice. The only red on these screens would be a genuine error the person caused.

Never blocks a send

There is no disabled button anywhere in this sheet. "Send anyway" is always the primary action, in brand green.

No "dismiss"

The marker is evidence with a date, not an accusation to argue with. It clears when the address is corrected, or it quiets with age. There is nothing to dismiss.

No technical word, anywhere

No bounce, no suppression, no delivery report, no DLR, no webhook, no carrier, no terminal status. A salon owner and a plumber read these screens.

Never emails about email

The notice never travels on the channel that failed. In-app and push, always.

Never tells the wrong person

A customer whose address is broken cannot read about it. The provider is told. A person's own address is the exception — they are told in the app.
