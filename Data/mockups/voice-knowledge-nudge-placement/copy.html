Why the reframe — what it already knows with zero documents

Every one of these is embedded in the call's opening prompt before a single knowledge document exists (RealtimeSessionPayloadBuilder.cs). The prompt calls the profile “complete and authoritative” and tells the model to answer from it instantly, never with a tool:

Business name Description Every service Spoken price per service Duration per service Weekly working hours Service areas Addresses Other branch locations Active offers Currency Online-booking flag Rating
So “knows 3 documents and 5 FAQs” describes a fraction of what it knows — and implicitly tells the provider their seven onboarding steps didn't count. Your own AI Knowledge page already gets this right: knowledge.pageSubtitle says “What your AI receptionist knows about your business beyond your services”. The nudge is the only place that lost that framing.

What's wrong with it
  • “know your business inside out” implies it doesn't — the same false framing you caught in the summary.
  • “The more it knows, the better it helps your callers” is a truism. The most valuable line says something the reader already assumes.
  • No example of a question it can't answer today — the one thing that would make a provider think “people do ask that”.
  • “inside out” is an idiom → translates badly into hi / gu / fr / es.
What's good, and kept
  • “in your own words” — the real differentiator. Kept in every proposal.
  • Naming the concrete document types (price lists, policies, spec sheets, FAQs) — they match the actual upload types in the feature.
  • Warm, non-technical register.

Why A2 over the i.e./etc. version
  • “your Clinket profile” is the whole set, so the dash-list reads as examples on its own — nothing has to signal incompleteness.
  • “etc.” reads as trailing off. In the one line whose job is reassurance, that costs confidence.
  • “i.e.” means that is, precisely; an example list needs “e.g.” The “etc.” proves they're examples.
  • No Latin abbreviations to survive translation into fr / es / hi / gu — hi and gu have no natural equivalent.
  • “profile” is the app's own word — the page is literally titled My Profile.
What your version was right about
  • Naming Clinket — anchors the knowledge to the account they set up. Adopted.
  • Including offers — factually in the profile payload (activeOffers) and their own sidebar label. Adopted.
  • Signalling the list isn't exhaustive — right instinct, achieved by the umbrella noun instead.
  • Honest note: 23 chars shorter still lands on 4 lines at 340px. The saving buys clarity, not a line.

Two defects fixed on the way through

live today
DefectWhereRenders asFix
The block shows when docs or FAQs exist, but the string always prints both counts Web + mobile knows 3 documents and 0 FAQs Three sentence shells picked in code — both / docs-only / FAQs-only. Handles every combination without nested ICU.
Mobile has no pluralization — the count is raw {{docCount}} interpolation Mobile only knows 1 documents and 1 FAQs i18next plural fragments (_one / _other) composed into the shell, matching the web ICU plurals. Option D removes the need entirely.
i18next can only pluralize on a single count per key, so two counts in one sentence can't be pluralized inline on mobile. Composing a pluralized fragment per noun is the standard way out — and it keeps each translated string short and natural, which matters given the earlier trouble with machine-translated ICU tokens.
