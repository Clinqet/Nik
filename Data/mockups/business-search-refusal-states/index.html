Amber, not red
Nothing is broken and nothing is lost. Red stays reserved for a real failure, so that when red appears it still means something.

Answers arrive a word at a time. On a long answer the reader is often part-way up, re-reading a line, while the rest lands below the fold — and nothing says so, so a half-written answer looks finished. On a short answer there is nothing to say, and saying it anyway is noise.

How it works, and why it looks like this

‼It waits until four lines are hidden
Not one pixel — four lines of the answer's own text. Two or three lines below the fold is not something a reader misses, and a mark that is always there stops being read at all. Four is the balance: enough that a paragraph is genuinely out of sight, few enough that a long answer is never left silent. Measured in LINES, not pixels, so it still holds when the provider has made the text bigger.
‼No new sentence at all — this is the cue the app already ships
The left-hand menu already does exactly this when it has more rows than fit: a soft fade at the edge that takes no press, and a round disc with a green arrow inside it. Same idea here, on white instead of navy. Nothing new to translate, and providers have already learnt it.
Never a solid green pill, and never text
Green filled means chosen everywhere else in the app — the menu's own note says so, which is why its disc is see-through rather than lime. A worded banner would also have to be translated five times to say what an arrow says on its own.
The green dot means “and it is still being written”
Disc alone: there is more to read. Disc with the dot and the soft ring: more is still arriving. That is the difference a sentence was trying to carry, done without words.
It disappears the moment it stops being true
At the end of the answer, and on any answer short enough to fit. A control that is always there and does nothing is what made the menu's first version read as broken.
On the phone it pulses three times, then holds
The same motion the knowledge screen already uses for “look here” — and it does not move at all if the phone asks for reduced motion.
The only words are for a screen reader
BusinessSearch.Answer.ScrollDown — “Go to the newest line”. Never shown on screen, exactly like the menu's own sidebar.scrollDown.
One shared number, so the two apps agree
ANSWER_CUE_MIN_HIDDEN_LINES = 4 lives beside the other shared rules, with a test that proves the computer and the phone read the same value.

Four sentences, written by the screen

The language is named in its own language
“ગુજરાતી”, not “Gujarati” — the person about to speak it reads it that way.
The countdown never shows zero
Whole seconds, and at zero the panel closes. A frozen “0” would look stuck.

The words, and where they come from

Three sentences, written by the screen
Insights.Chart.AmountInCurrency — “{amount} in {currency}”. And the amber note below the amounts: Insights.Chart.CountedApart — “Counted apart, never added” — with Insights.Chart.CountedApartBody. Added 2026-09-07: the note was drawn in this sheet from the start and this panel did not list it, so it was nearly not built at all.
‼The currency's name needs no new words at all
Both the computer and the phone can already name a currency in the reader's own language, so “CAD” becomes “Canadian dollars” in English and “dollars canadiens” in French. Where a device cannot, the three-letter code shows instead — which is what appears on every invoice anyway.
The amount stays bold, the name stays plain
The figure is still what the eye lands on. On the phone the pairs go one per line, never squeezed onto one where a symbol could be read against the wrong number.
Old bars are dimmed while new dates load
So a stale chart is never read as a fresh one.
A chart that fails is amber
The earnings are fine; only the picture is missing. Red would say the money was wrong.
