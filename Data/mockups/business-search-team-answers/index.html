When the answer is about your team's work

Ask Clinket can already answer from what the business wrote down. This sheet is what it looks like when the answer is about work instead — bookings, quotes, invoices, leads, customers, messages, reviews, the calls Clinket answered, and money a customer has asked back. Everything here is bounded by what the person asking is allowed to see, so most of the sheet is about saying that out loud.

The one idea this whole sheet is built on

An answer about work must always say how much of the business it looked at and whose work it counted. A number with neither is a number the provider will act on and cannot check. Every state below is a way of saying one of those two things.

01 The answer says what it actually counted

The same question, asked by two people in the same business on the same day, is answered with two different numbers — correctly. What must never differ is whether the answer says which it is. The sentence is written by Clinket with the answer, not stitched together on screen.

“This covers your own work only” is Clinket's sentence, not the screen's

It arrives inside the answer, in the language the member is reading in, so it sits in the paragraph it belongs to instead of as a separate banner the eye skips. The screen adds nothing.

The quiet line under the answer is the screen's, and it is a summary, not a claim

It names where the answer came from and, when it covered only your own work, that too. It never carries a count — a total is Clinket's to state or to withhold.

“My” narrows, and never widens

An owner who asks for my bookings gets their own, even though they can see everything. An owner who asks for all bookings gets the business. A technician gets their own either way — the word cannot reach past what they are allowed to see.

02 “Nothing assigned to you” is not “the business has none”

These two sentences look alike and mean opposite things. A technician told “there are no bookings this week” may reasonably go home. The distinction is decided by Clinket, which knows which of the two it measured — the screen never guesses it from an empty list.

Empty because nothing is yours

Right

Nothing is assigned to you this week, 1–7 September.

The business may still have work booked — this only covers jobs assigned to you. Ask your manager if you were expecting something.

Empty because the business has none

Right

There are no bookings anywhere in the business this week, 1–7 September.

Nothing is booked for these dates.

✗ Never

The same words for both
You have no bookings this week.

True for a technician with an empty queue and for a business with nothing booked. One of those two readers will act on it wrongly.

✓ Always

Two sentences, decided by Clinket
Nothing is assigned to you this week. —— There are no bookings anywhere in the business this week.

The screen renders whichever arrives. It has no rule of its own, because from an empty list the two are indistinguishable.

03 A refusal that names nobody, and confirms nothing

Two different refusals, and neither may leak. Asking about money you cannot see must not reveal whether there is any; asking about a colleague must not reveal whether that colleague exists. Both sentences are fixed — Clinket does not compose them, and no record is read before either is said.

A person who is not on the team gets the same words as a person who is

If someone asks about “Priya” and no Priya works here, the reply is the identical sentence. Anything else turns Ask Clinket into a way of finding out who works at a business.

Nothing is looked up before a refusal

The refusal is decided from who is asking, not from what exists. So a refusal is instant, costs nothing, and cannot be timed to infer an answer.

04 When a colleague is named, the answer names them back

A manager can ask about a teammate's work. Clinket has to work out which teammate — from a first name, a nickname, or the name written in another alphabet. It gets this right almost every time, and the one way it goes wrong is quiet: for a person who does not work here, it can reach for the nearest name that does. So the answer always says which person it used.

Right person

The name is in the answer, not only in the question

Wrong person — and now it is visible

The provider can see the mistake in one glance

3 bookings are assigned to Gauri Desai on 1 September.

The provider asked about “Gaurav”. Because the answer says Gauri Desai, the mistake is obvious and correctable. Without the name it would have been three confident, wrong bookings.

Assigned to, not sent by

Clinket records who a job is assigned to. It does not record who sent a quote out. So the answer to “how many quotes did Gaurav send?” says “assigned to Gaurav Shah” in the sentence itself — the number is right, and the provider can see exactly what it counts.

Two people with the same first name

The answer asks which one, listing only the people this member is allowed to see, and counts nothing until they say. A guess between two real colleagues is worse than a question.

Naming a colleague is not the same as being allowed to

A member who may only see their own work can write any name they like; they still get their own work and the fixed sentence from section 03. The name never widens what is returned.

A member who has left

Treated as not found — the same reply as a name that was never here. Their past work keeps their name on it, exactly as every other list in the app already shows it.

05 Dates — and why the answer always repeats them

“Yesterday” is decided by Clinket using the business's own clock, not the phone's and not the server's. A provider in Ahmedabad asking at 00:30 means the day that just ended there. The answer always states the days it used, in words, so a wrong day is visible rather than invisible.

07 Calls Clinket answered — and the honesty this card owes

“What did I miss?” is the most common thing a provider asks on a Monday morning. Clinket answers the calls they missed and writes a short note about each one. That note is Clinket's own summary, not the caller's words — and a summary can be wrong. So the card says which it is, every time, and never lets the answer present it as a quote.

Opening the call is one tap away, and that page already decides who may listen. Ask Clinket adds no new way to reach a recording — the same rule as documents, where no file is ever handed over.

10 The chips — work questions come back

Sheet M4 removed “Show my bookings this week” and “How many leads did we get last week?” for one reason only: nothing could answer them. Now something can. They return — but only for a member who is allowed to be answered, so a chip still cannot dead-end.

ChipShown whenWhy it cannot dead-end
How much is a {service}? the catalogue has at least one service unchanged from M4 — the name came from their own catalogue
What's on this week? the member can see bookings answers with their own week, or the honest “nothing is assigned to you” from section 02 — both are real answers
How many leads did we get last week? the member can see leads a count always exists, including nought
What are our opening hours? always — reserved, never dropped to make room unchanged from M4
What did I miss on the phone? not a chip a business without the phone assistant has nothing to answer with, and the chip would advertise a feature they do not have
Any refund requests? not a chip most members cannot see them, and for the few who can it is a question they ask when they have a reason to — not a suggestion

Order, and the cap

Their own service names first, then the work chips, then hours. Four on web, three on a phone. Hours is still reserved and is added after the cap, so it can never be squeezed out.

‼️ This revises M4 section 01b and partly restores M1

M4's row stands for a member who cannot see work; the two work chips are added for a member who can. M1's original row still does not return in full — its “What is our cancellation policy?” assumes a document exists and stays out.

While the member's access is still being read, only the safe chips appear

Same rule M4 set for the catalogue: chips do not pop in late, and a work chip is never shown to someone who would then be refused.

11 A business of one person

Most providers work alone. For them none of the team wording above should ever appear: there is nobody to be assigned to, nobody to name, and nothing to be restricted from. The answer is simply the answer.

Business with a team

Worth saying whose work it was

You have 3 bookings assigned to you this week, 1–7 September. This covers your own work only.

One-person business

No such sentence at all

You have 3 bookings this week, 1–7 September.

Saying “assigned to you” to somebody who is the whole business reads as though something was withheld.

Nothing on screen changes

No chip is removed, no control is hidden, no message appears. A solo provider should not be able to tell that a team version of this exists.

Asking about a person, in a business of one

Clinket is never offered names to match against, so it cannot invent a colleague. A question with a name in it is answered as the provider's own work.

The moment a second person joins

The “your own work” sentence starts appearing on its own, with no setting to turn on. It is decided fresh on every question, because a teammate can be added between one question and the next.

No “record” jargon, no “query”, no “filter”, no “scope”, no “permission”, no “tool”. The words on this sheet are the words a provider already uses: bookings, quotes, invoices, leads, customers, messages, reviews, calls, refunds, your own work, the business.

13 What this sheet settles

1 — Every work answer states what it counted

Whose work, and which days. Both inside Clinket's own sentence, in the member's language.

2 — An empty queue and an empty business are two different sentences

Decided by Clinket, never inferred on screen from a list with nothing in it.

3 — A refusal names nobody and confirms nothing

The same words whether the person exists or not, and nothing is looked up first.

4 — When a colleague is named, the answer names them back

So the one way this can go wrong is visible in the first line instead of hidden in a number.

5 — Work cards are the document card's shape, with one action into the app
