Assistant voice — the two best voices first, and samples in your language

The picker leads with Marin and Cedar, the voices that sound most natural on a phone call, and pre-selects the first one for a new assistant. Each voice plays a ten-second sample in the language the provider chose for their callers. The descriptions now say how each voice really sounds, so a provider looking for "calm" is no longer steered to a weaker voice. Web and the provider phone app ship together.

4 · Provider phone app

The app complements its own form rather than copying the web. The chosen voice stays on the form with its own play button, and "Choose another voice" opens a bottom sheet, the same pattern the form already uses for the main language. Tapping a row chooses it, plays it and gives a light tap of haptic feedback. Playback stops when the sheet closes, when the screen is left, and when the app goes to the background. The samples ship inside the app, so they also play offline.

6 · What you are approving

Order and default

The server's list decides both

The voice list on the server now starts Marin, Cedar, then the rest as they are today. A new assistant, or one saved without a voice, starts on the first voice in that list. A saved voice is never changed. If the list arrives after the form, the default is applied once, only while nothing is chosen.

The badge

"Recommended" comes from the server, not from the screen

Each voice in the server's list gets a recommended yes/no, sent to both apps, so moving the badge is a setting change and never an app release. Only Marin and Cedar are marked.

Samples

Ten seconds, in the caller's language

Sixty new samples (ten voices × English, French, Spanish, Hindi, Punjabi, Gujarati), recorded with the new assistant model and matched in loudness. The main language picks which one plays; English when none is chosen. Hindi and Punjabi use the female or male wording to match the voice. The old three-second English samples are removed.

Playing

One at a time, and it always stops

Choosing, changing the main language, leaving the page, closing the sheet or sending the app to the background stops the sample. A failure is said in plain words, never silent.

Words

Honest descriptions, the names untranslated

"Calm and steady" (Ash) becomes "Deep and steady", "Gentle and reassuring" (Sage) becomes "Soft and light", and Marin is now "Warm and calm". Voice names stay as names in every language (Spanish and French had translated "Cedar"). The phone app stops showing the server's English label.

Accessibility

Radio buttons, readable names, a navy focus ring

The voices are a radio group (arrow keys on web), each play button is named "Play Marin's sample" / "Stop the sample" in the provider's language, targets are 44 × 44, and text contrast is AA on both themes.

7 · Every word, and where it comes from

All screen copy; none comes from the server any more. Web keys live in public/lang/{en-US,es-US,fr-CA,gu-IN,hi-IN}.json, app keys in src/Locales/{en,es,fr,gu,hi}.json, in all five languages. {language} is the language's own name, written by the device. French and Spanish use the feminine form because "voix" and "voz" are feminine; Gujarati uses the masculine because "અવાજ" is masculine.
